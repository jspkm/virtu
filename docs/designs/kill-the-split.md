## Problem Statement

The session opened with a proposed pivot: an IMSLP or MuseScore-scale library in the
back, plus it-just-works sync and cloud storage, to justify an annual subscription.
That framing did not survive the diagnostic. What replaced it is sharper and is
grounded in the one real musician attached to this project.

**Hannah, a working classical cellist, currently annotates her scores in GoodNotes or
Notability, because palm rejection works there, and then lives with clumsy page turns.
Her scores and setlists live in forScore. Her markings live somewhere else.**

She runs two apps for one score. Her library, her programmes, and her page turns are in
one place; her fingerings, bowings, and interpretive decisions are in another. They do
not talk to each other, and every new piece she studies gets split the same way, forever.

The founder's own framing, stated late in the session: **a score is not a document, it is
where a musician stores what they have learned.** forScore treats accumulated markings as
ink on a PDF. Virtu should treat them as knowledge.

**Relationship to PRD §0.1.** The one-sentence pitch, "a music reader you can actually
write in," **stands unchanged for v1 scoping**, and every decision in this block still
resolves against it. The knowledge-repository framing is positioning for v1.x and beyond.
It is recorded here as a proposed amendment to §0.1, not as a silent replacement, and it
changes nothing that ships in this block.

## Status Quo

The thing Virtu is actually replacing, in her hands, today:

| Job | Tool | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Store and organise scores | forScore ($24.99 one-time) | none, works |
| Read and turn pages | forScore | none, works |
| **Write on the music** | **GoodNotes / Notability** | **the split** |
| Get a PDF in | Files, email, browser | a few taps |
| Keep markings safe | forScore base-tier sync, or nothing | free, invisible |

The friction is not any one step. It is that step three lives in a different app from
steps one and two, so a marked page is severed from the library that should own it, and
the accumulated knowledge of a career is scattered across note-taking documents.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**The named human:** Hannah. Working classical cellist. Owns an iPad and a Pencil, has
used forScore for years, is openly unimpressed by it, and already votes with her hands by
writing in GoodNotes instead. Consequence she faces: her markings are her craft, and they
are stored where her music is not.

**Second user, currently unnamed:** her colleagues and her teachers. This is a real gap.
Nobody outside the family has been named, and the social-default finding means the second
user is a strategic question, not a marketing one.

**The narrowest wedge:** be the one app where the score and the markings live together,
with writing that feels as good as GoodNotes. Not a library. Not storage. The end of the
split.

## Constraints

Carried from PRD §0 and §10, all binding on this block:

- **Fingers never draw.** Pencil-only is a default, not a preference. (§0.2) *Text
  annotation collides with this: positioning a caret wants a finger tap. Item 4 must
  resolve it as a **proposed §0.2 amendment requiring an explicit §0 decision**, on the
  same protocol as the §0.1 amendment above. It is not a spec detail.*
- **A stroke is never lost.** Durable within 250 ms of pencil-up. No Save button, no
  "restore unsaved changes?" dialog. Ship late rather than ship this wrong. (§0.3) *This
  block funds the §8.4 crash-durability harness rather than merely quoting the rule.*
- **Local-first always.** Works in airplane mode on a dark stage. (§0.4)
- **Simpler than the incumbent, not richer.** Every feature must help someone play from,
  or write on, the page in front of them. (§0.6)
- **No recognition engine in v1.** (§0.7)
- **No third-party dependencies in v1.** (§10) *Consequence: no Sentry. See item 12 for
  what that actually leaves you with, which is less than it sounds.*

**The rendering situation, stated as the code and `docs/TESTPLAN.md` have it.** Two
earlier drafts of this document got this wrong in opposite directions. This version matches
TESTPLAN's "Known OS constraints (iPadOS 26.x — do not re-litigate)" section, which is
authoritative:

> **The inking pipeline is entirely ours.** `PencilObserverRecognizer`
> (`ReadingPageView.swift:8`) captures pencil input, `WetStrokeView` (`:142`) draws the
> live stroke, and `InkRenderer` draws committed ink for both display and export.
> `applyInputGate()` (`:418`) disables PencilKit's drawing recognizer whenever an ink tool
> is armed, with the comment: "PencilKit draws nothing of ours any more: its recognizer
> runs only for the tools whose interaction it still owns (eraser, lasso)."
> **PencilKit owns exactly two things:** the eraser and lasso interactions, and the display
> during a lasso session (`canvasAlphaForTool = tool is PKLassoTool ? 1 : 0.02`, `:475`).

**The hazard text lands in is therefore the lasso/eraser handoff and the canvas rebuild,
not an inking handoff.** `scheduleCanvasNormalization(after: 0.25)` (`:297`) calls
`rebuildCanvas()` (`:258`) on a delay after a gesture, guarded by `!isLassoSession`,
destroying and recreating the canvas. Anything living on that canvas dies with it.

**The architectural decision that follows, stated here so it is not made by accident.**
Text uses the same wet/dry split the ink already uses: **a text mark is rasterized into the
committed ink layer at rest** (and is therefore soft at zoom, per PRD §7.4), **and a
transient editing overlay exists only while an edit session is open, torn down on commit** —
exactly what `WetStrokeView` already does for a live stroke. That is not a new display
owner. The exclusion later in this document means no *persistent* vector text layer. PRD
§7.1 calls the two-owner problem the largest source of bugs in the app; a third persistent
owner is the thing this block must not build by accident.

## Premises

Agreed after debate, including one the founder disputed and one the second opinion
overturned.

1. **The individual reader slot was contested, and forScore won every time.** Revised
   during the session. MobileSheets, Piascore, unrealBook, Nomad Play, Enote, and Newzik's
   individual tier all took runs at it. This is a graveyard, not an empty field. The
   graveyard is a market signal and deserves respect.
2. **Nobody contested the physical writing surface specifically.** Newzik went
   institutional (admin creates groups, conductor pushes marks down). nkoda sells licensed
   editions by subscription. Henle sells its own Urtext catalogue. None competed for the
   individual musician's daily writing experience.
3. **The strongest substitute is not a music app.** It is GoodNotes and Notability, and
   Hannah is already using them. This both validates the pain and warns that a free,
   widely known workaround already exists. **Untested beyond N=1. See The Assignment.**
4. **The score is a knowledge repository.** The differentiated thesis for v1.x+. It
   reframes sync as preservation, sharing as teaching, and cross-edition mark transfer
   (§M5) as the actual point rather than a clever feature.
5. **Capability wins the individual musician; distribution decides the rate of spread.**
   Both are product problems. Only the first is a coding problem.
6. **AI-assisted build speed is real, already proven in this repo, and is not the binding
   constraint.** It produced annotation layers, four nib widths, four line styles, Stage
   mode, and programmes, all past M1 scope, with zero users. The bottleneck was never how
   fast features could be written. **Consequence: this document prices work in calendar
   weeks including hardware sessions, Apple latency, and Hannah's availability. Not in
   generated-code hours.**

## Approaches Considered

Priced in **calendar weeks**, per premise 6, including hardware sessions, Apple review
latency, and Hannah's availability.

### Approach A: The door
Complete PDF ingestion, one-tap send of a marked page, and a TestFlight build out with
crash reporting. **~1 calendar week** (most of it Apple provisioning and Beta App Review
latency, not code). Risk low. Answers the day-14 retention question soonest. Does nothing
about the GoodNotes split, so a musician who needs text cannot actually switch.

### Approach B: The knowledge layer
Dated marking history per piece, teaching send as a first-class flow, the beginnings of
mark portability across editions. §M5 brought forward. **~2 months.** Risk high: §0.3
exposure the moment marks move between documents or people. The only path that honestly
justifies an annual price, but it means designing a knowledge layer before watching a
single musician's markings accumulate.

### Approach C: Kill the split — SELECTED
Close the gap that sends her to GoodNotes, then ship. **~5-6 calendar weeks**, re-priced
upward twice under adversarial review. Round one found four omitted sub-systems: journal v3
and its migration, the App Group container for a share extension, Beta App Review latency,
and the TESTPLAN extension. Round two found that the text work itself was under-priced at
two weeks, that the crash-durability harness is a build rather than a run, and that the
zoom-sharpness question is a display-architecture decision rather than a sub-item. Risk is
medium-to-high and concentrated in one place: **text annotation must be built from zero
inside the two-owner display path the PRD calls the largest source of bugs in the app.**
PencilKit offers no text primitive to borrow. That risk is why the block opens with a spike
and a branch rule rather than a commitment.

### Ruled out during the session
- **v2 backup and restore as a paid tier.** forScore ships automatic device sync and
  backup in its base app. A backup you never needed is not a renewal pitch.
- **v3 in-app score library as a subscription.** nkoda holds a six-figure catalogue of
  licensed editions from major classical publishers (verified by category search,
  2026-08-21, nkoda.com — re-check before acting on it). Those are publisher relationships
  a solo builder cannot obtain. IMSLP is already free in a browser. Kept on the table only
  as a possible acquisition channel, never as a billing justification.
- **v4 shared markings as originally scoped.** Newzik has shipped conductor-to-section
  push, per-musician private layers, and rehearsal-dated archiving since 2016. The
  surviving version is peer-to-peer teaching transfer, not an ensemble system, and it
  collides with §0.3 the moment marks merge.
- **Free forever to gain market share.** TestFlight **defers** the pricing decision at zero
  cost; it does not test free as a price, since builds expire after 90 days, require an
  Apple ID and the TestFlight app, and are not discoverable. PRD §13 already proposes
  $24.99 one-time plus a free tier capped at three works, which is the actual pricing
  design and is not reopened here.

## Recommended Approach

**Selected by the founder: Approach C, kill the split first**, with complete PDF ingestion
folded in as a prerequisite rather than treated as a separate option.

The founder's reasoning is sound and stronger than the advisor's counter-proposal: without
text annotation, Hannah cannot stop using GoodNotes, so a TestFlight build shipped today
would test a product she structurally cannot switch to. The day-14 retention experiment
would be rigged to fail. Closing the gap first makes the eventual test meaningful.

**The cost of this choice, stated plainly.** Approach A reaches the M1 answer in roughly
three weeks (one to build, two to observe). Approach C reaches it in roughly **eight to
nine**: six weeks of block, two of observation, plus Beta App Review latency, and one more
if item 10's five-day fix cap is spent. **Choosing C defers M1's gate by five to six
weeks.** That gate has never been evaluated since M1 shipped, and
PRD §14 names the failure to evaluate it as the project's central process problem. PRD §6
says do not begin the next milestone until the current gate is met. **That rule is being
overridden here by explicit decision, recorded as such, not by drift.** The branch rules
below are what keep the override honest.

**Advisor dissent, recorded not relitigated:** two weeks of watching her use the current
build would sharpen what gets built here. The founder's counter-argument addresses it
directly and he has the domain contact.

## The Block

Sequenced, with gates. **Total: 6 calendar weeks, 7 if item 10's fix cap is spent.**
Re-priced upward three times under adversarial review. Do not run these in parallel except where marked.

### The one branch rule, stated once

Several gates below can send the block "back to Approach A." That means exactly this:

> **Drop items 4, 5 and 7, and the text-specific half of item 8. Everything else still
> ships: items 1, 2, 3, 6, 8 (non-text), 9, 10, 11, 12.** Library hygiene, the dead rails,
> the crash harness (item 6) and the run of it (item 9), and the ink A/B are wanted under

### Week 0 — evidence and paperwork, in parallel

**0a. The Assignment (see below).** Ask Hannah plus 8 of her colleagues about the GoodNotes
split.
- **Denominator:** 8 completed colleague conversations. Hannah is not in the count; she is
  the known yes.
- **The binary:** does this person annotate scores outside their reader at least weekly?
- **Branch: ≥5 of 8 → proceed. 3-4 of 8 → one re-interview round of 5, drawn from a
  narrower segment (her own section, her teachers' students), time-boxed to five days;
  ≥3 of 5 proceeds, otherwise Approach A. ≤2 of 8 → back to Approach A.**

**0b. Apple paperwork.** Bundle ID and team already exist (`com.jspkm.virtu`, team
`DQ56JFGV59` in `project.yml`). What is **missing** and each of which is a classic one-day
stall:
- App Store Connect app record.
- **App Group identifier**, plus `.entitlements` files for both targets. The repo has zero
  `.entitlements` files today and `project.yml` has no `entitlements` key.
- Regenerated provisioning profiles for two targets.
- Export-compliance declaration (`ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption`).
- TestFlight "Test Information": beta description and feedback email. Blocks external
  testing.
- **Hannah cannot be an internal tester** — internal testers must be App Store Connect
  users on the team. Plan for external testing from build 1, which means **Beta App Review,
  1-3 days, rejectable.**

**0c. Confirm the shape of the commitment, not the appetite.** She has already asked for a
build and been promised one, so the question is no longer whether she wants it. Confirm two
specifics: **that she will put her next assigned piece into it rather than trying it on an
old one**, and **that she will stay in it for two weeks before judging.**
- **Branch: if she will not commit to the two weeks, that is not a stop. Ship anyway and
  observe what she does; the request itself is the stronger signal.**

**0d. Text-rendering spike, 2 days.** **In `ReadingPageView.swift` (708 lines, the display
ownership state machine), not in `InkRenderer.swift`** — the latter is 181 lines of static
`CGContext` rasterization with a fully `PKDrawing`-typed API and no hit-testing, no
recognizers, no normalization. Four binary questions:
  Two of these are **decisions with a cost estimate**, not experiments. Do not let them
  fire the branch rule:
  - (a) *Decision.* `rebuildCanvas()` destroys only the canvas subview, so an editing
    overlay placed as a sibling of `inkView`/`wetView` survives by construction. Confirm
    the placement; do not treat it as an unknown.
  - (c) *Decision.* `InkRenderer`'s API is fully `PKDrawing`-typed (`draw(_ drawing:
    PKDrawing…)`, `image(for: [PKDrawing]…)`, `:80,158,165,178`), consumed by
    `renderInkFallback()` and `ExportButton`. The literal answer is always no, so as a gate
    it would abort the block on day two. Instead: **enumerate the `InkRenderer` and journal
    call sites a text record touches and estimate the refactor. Over one day, escalate.**

  These two are genuine hardware unknowns and **do** carry the branch rule:
  - (b) Can a text run be hit-tested without `canvas.isUserInteractionEnabled` stealing the
    touch?
  - (d) Can text coexist with the lasso session's `inkView.isHidden = true` and with
    `PKEraserTool`, which hit-tests `PKStroke`s and is structurally blind to a text record?
  - (e) **Zoom scale, promoted here from Week 2:** `PageRenderer.swift:90-95` rasterizes at
    `UIScreen.main.scale × fit-height` and zoom scales that bitmap. There is no way to
    render text at device scale *inside* a zoom-1 raster. The only two implementations are
    re-rasterizing the whole ink layer on every zoom change (a change to the hot path) or a
    live vector text layer outside the bitmap, **which is a third display owner** and needs
    its own handling in `ExportButton` flattening, in `stageRemapped()`, and in the margin
    export. **Default answer: ship text with zoom softness as a stated known defect.** The
    third owner is the thing this block must not build by accident.
  - **Branch: fail (b) or (d) → back to Approach A, and text becomes its own block with
    its own architecture decision.** This is the M0 spike question in a new costume, and
    M0's answer reshaped the architecture.

### Week 1 — the door

**1. Complete PDF ingestion.** Today Virtu cannot receive a PDF from another app at all:
there is no `CFBundleDocumentTypes` anywhere, and `LibraryView.swift:71`'s `fileImporter`
is the only entry point.
  - `CFBundleDocumentTypes` with `LSItemContentTypes = ["com.adobe.pdf"]`,
    `CFBundleTypeRole = Viewer`, and `LSHandlerRank`. **Neither `UTImportedTypeDeclarations`
    nor `UTExportedTypeDeclarations` is needed:** those declare types the system may not
    know about (imported = owned by someone else, exported = originated by you), and
    `com.adobe.pdf` is a system UTI. Declaring it produces a plist entry that does nothing.
  - **Bundle-ID trap.** `project.yml` sets `bundleIdPrefix: com.virtu` while the app
    overrides to `com.jspkm.virtu`. A share-extension target added without an explicit
    `PRODUCT_BUNDLE_IDENTIFIER` gets `com.virtu.VirtuShare` and will not match the App ID or
    the App Group under team `DQ56JFGV59`. Set it explicitly.
  - **No code sharing.** The extension writes the file and exits, so no framework target is
    created and PRD §10's `VirtuKit` (today an empty directory) stays unbuilt. That is the
    reason the container design above is cheap.
  - **All plist keys go into `project.yml`'s `info.properties`, never into
    `Virtu/Info.plist` directly.** XcodeGen writes that file from the YAML and will clobber
    hand edits the moment the share-extension target is added and the project regenerated.
  - `LSSupportsOpeningDocumentsInPlace`, an `.onOpenURL` handler (the repo has none), and
    `start/stopAccessingSecurityScopedResource` for in-place files.
  - Share-extension target in `project.yml` (today: two targets, Virtu and VirtuTests),
    with its `NSExtensionActivationRule`.
  - Drag-and-drop onto the library grid.
  - **A failure alert** naming the file and the reason (unreadable, encrypted, not a PDF).
    PRD §M3 records that a failed import currently prints to the console and tells the
    musician nothing. This block triples the number of import doors; funding item 2's
    argument and not this one is inconsistent.
  - **Success includes AirDrop**, which comes free with the document types and must be
    tested rather than assumed.

> **Container design, decided here to avoid a migration:** the share extension writes
> **only the incoming PDF** to the App Group container. The journal and the SwiftData store
> stay where they are, in the app container, and the app imports on next launch. Moving
> `StrokeJournal`'s root would be a data migration on the one subsystem §0.3 protects. Do
> not do it for a share extension.

> **Deferred-import UX, which that design creates and which nobody has designed.** PRD §11
> mandates a confirmation sheet that always appears and cannot be skipped. So: she shares
> from forScore, nothing visibly happens, and two days later she opens Virtu mid-rehearsal
> in Stage mode and gets a modal metadata sheet. Specify in item 1: **when the pending
> sheet is presented (deferred while Stage mode or Perform is active), the queue ordering
> for multiple pending PDFs, how to discard one, and what the extension shows on success.**

**2. Library hygiene, PRD open P0s.** Delete, rename, and edit-metadata for a work. Today a
mis-typed import is permanent and unremovable. On the day-14 path she imports her next
assigned piece, fat-fingers the mandatory metadata sheet, and can never fix it. That reads
as a retention signal when it is a missing button.

**3. Remove *both* dead rail destinations for this build.** `MainView.swift:104`
(`FindStubView`, "Coming in M1") and `MainView.swift:119` (`ToolsStubView`, "Debug panel —
M1"). PRD §5: two of five rail destinations lead nowhere, and a dead end at the stand costs
more than a missing feature. **Not purely deletion:** removing Find drops the only home for
PRD §5's Find surface, which is a §5 scope change to record, and the ghost-sliver rail's
geometry changes with two fewer destinations.

**3b. Ship the interim TestFlight build at the end of Week 1.** She asked for a build ASAP
and was promised one; items 0b, 1, 2 and 3 produce a shippable one. Send it. It costs the
archive and upload plus Beta App Review, both of which are on the Week 0-1 path anyway, and
it converts a six-week silence into a kept promise.

> **What the interim build does and does not test.** It cannot test the split, because text
> is not in it, so **day-14 retention against this build is not the M1 gate and must not be
> read as one.** What it does buy: the import path exercised by a real musician on real
> repertoire, the first crash data, confirmation that the promise held, and an early read
> on whether anything outside text drives her back to forScore. If she stays in it *without*
> text, that is a finding worth more than the whole block.

### Weeks 2-4 — text annotation, the actual work

Re-priced from two weeks to three.

**Correction that makes this cheaper than an earlier draft assumed:** text is largely
**simulator-verifiable**. The inking pipeline is ours, and `PencilObserverRecognizer`
normalizes absent force to 0.6 specifically "so the simulator draws at full nib width"
(`ReadingPageView.swift:10,21`). Placement, editing, keyboard entry, journal round-trip,
layer isolation, Stage remap and export flattening all work in the simulator. Only
pencil-specific gestures (tap-to-place with a Pencil, drag-to-move) need hardware. **Batch
the hardware sessions; do not assume a device round-trip per iteration.**

**4. Write a one-page text-annotation spec before any code. One day, with a ceiling.**
PRD §M2 lists the word "text" in a tool list. That is not a specification. Run `/spec`.

  **Bounded by default, so the spec cannot sprawl:** one bundled font (Archivo), three
  sizes, the four preset inks, tap-to-place, tap-to-edit, drag-to-move. **Not in v1:**
  rotation, rich text, per-character styling, bounding-box resize handles.

  Must decide, and each of these will otherwise be decided by accident:
  - **§0.2 conflict, with a cheap default that probably dissolves it.** A caret wants a
    tap. **Default: a *pencil* tap places the caret**, as it does in GoodNotes.
    **Correction from the outside voice:** this is NOT "just another `armedInking == nil`
    case." `applyInputGate()` (`:418`) sets
    `canvas.drawingGestureRecognizer.isEnabled = live && armedInking == nil`, so nil *hands
    input to PencilKit* (eraser and lasso), and `inkGestureBegan` (`:311`) guards
    `armedInking != nil`. **Text needs its own armed state and its own arbitration with
    scrolling.** Only if hardware testing shows pencil-tap placement is
    unusable does this become a **proposed §0.2 amendment needing an explicit §0 decision**
    on the §0.1 protocol. Do not put a §0 governance cycle on the critical path by default.
  - **§0.3 commit rule.** There is no pencil-up event for typing. Name the durability
    trigger and its budget.
  - **The Right Page.** Superseded by eng review Issue 8: the surface at
    `marginRightIndex = -1` is not a margin, it is a **full writable Page belonging to the
    score** (one page wide in portrait, two in landscape). So this is not a special case:
    text on the Right Page is text on a page, at page sizing. The bottom surface
    (`marginBottomIndex = -2`) is **non-writable scroll headroom** and takes no text
    decision at all. See "Eng Review Amendments" below.
  - **Lasso and eraser.** Both are PencilKit tools hit-testing `PKStroke`s, and a
    non-`PKDrawing` text record is **structurally invisible to both**. So "lasso a mark and
    drag it" (TESTPLAN half 2 step 4, ROADMAP M4) will silently not apply to text, and the
    eraser — the far more common gesture — cannot delete it. Decide both: either text is
    out of lasso and eraser scope in v1 and the UI says so, with an explicit delete
    affordance instead, or a parallel selection and erase mechanism is specified and priced.
  - **Keyboard versus Scribble. Default: software and hardware keyboard only; Scribble is
    excluded from v1.** `UIScribbleInteraction` competes directly with
    `PencilObserverRecognizer` (`ReadingPageView.swift:198-207`), which claims pencil
    touches on the page.
  - Layer isolation across layers 1-10, Stage-mode colour remap, undo integration, export
    flattening.

**5. Build text annotation.** Includes **journal v3**: a non-`PKDrawing` annotation record
(`StrokeJournal.swift:16` is at `schemaVersion: 2`, storing `PKDrawing.dataRepresentation()`
per part/layer/page).

> **v3 is read-forward, like v2. There is no rewrite pass over stored ink, ever.**
> `StrokeJournal.swift:96-98` refuses migration by design and says why in the code: "a
> rewrite pass over every page of every part is exactly the moment a crash would cost
> someone their bowings." ROADMAP's "one migration, not two" means one *format change*, not
> a rewrite pass. v1 and v2 records are decoded in place on load. Do not instruct otherwise.

### Accepted corrections from the outside voice — no decision, fold into the spec

Factual gaps, verified against source. Each amends the item named.

- **Journal v3 is not automatically read-forward** (amends item 5). Adding a non-optional
  field to a synthesized `Codable` record makes existing v2 JSON **fail to decode**: a
  property default does not supply a missing key. v3 needs an explicit `init(from:)` using
  `decodeIfPresent`, plus fixture tests carrying **actual v2 bytes**, not v2-shaped objects.
- **Hardware keyboard entry collides with page turns** (amends item 4).
  `ReadingSurfaceView.swift:734` claims `" "` and the arrow keys as `UIKeyCommand` with
  `wantsPriorityOverSystemBehavior = true`. Typing a space turns the page. Suspend them for
  the duration of a text session.
- **The pending-import queue needs an atomic protocol** (amends item 1). The extension and
  the foreground drain can observe the same directory concurrently: interrupted copies,
  partially written PDFs, duplicate drains, filename collisions, orphaned files. Use
  temp-write-then-rename, or a manifest. The existing importer also leaves its copied PDF
  behind when metadata confirmation is cancelled and silently ignores a SwiftData save
  failure (`LibraryView.swift:304`, `ImportMetadataSheet.swift:89`).
- **The Right Page needs a canonical coordinate system** (amends Issue 8). "One page wide in
  portrait, two in landscape" does not say whether portrait *clips* a two-page canvas,
  *scales* it, or *reflows* it. Journal page size, rotation, hit-testing and export all
  depend on the answer, and getting it wrong stretches or relocates existing marks when the
  iPad rotates. Decide it with the rename, in Week 1.
- **The crash harness needs an execution architecture** (amends item 6). The project has
  only an app target and a hosted unit-test target (`project.yml:9`); calling
  `kill(getpid())` there kills the test runner. It needs a test-instrumented app launch, an
  external relaunch driver, deterministic fault points, and an explicit ruling on whether a
  crash inside the 250 ms window may lose the latest operation.
- **Persistence lacks lifecycle semantics, not just error handling** (amends item 5 and
  Issue 4). Startup replay is async while page loading is sync; saves have no completion or
  flush; export can read the *previous* compacted record immediately after a mark; and
  `deleteAll` (`StrokeJournal.swift:105`) iterates real page indices and **ignores the Right
  Page's negative slot**, so deleting a work orphans its Right Page ink.
- **Do not casually "wire up" the orphaned `AnnotationLayer` model** (amends item 5). Wiring
  it creates two persistence authorities, SwiftData and the journal, with no atomic
  transaction between them, which *enlarges* the stroke-loss surface. Delete it safely with
  a `VersionedSchema` and `MigrationPlan`, or leave it dormant. Do not wire it.

### The Right Page is a printable sheet — new constraint from the founder

**The Right Page will be printed often, in standard sizes, initially Letter and A4.**
That changes it from a writable surface into a sheet of paper with a fixed physical shape,
and it settles the coordinate-system question the eng review's outside voice raised.

**D1 — Geometry. DECIDED: score height wins, width derives from paper proportion.**

| | Portrait | Landscape |
|---|---|---|
| Score shows | one page | two-page spread |
| Right Page is | **one portrait sheet** | **one landscape sheet** |
| Height | the score's height | the score's height |
| Width | height × 0.773 (Letter) or × 0.707 (A4) | height ÷ 0.773 or ÷ 0.707 |
| Approx width | ~0.77× height | ~1.29× height |

It stays flush with the spread, which is what variant A gets visually right, **and** it is
a true Letter or A4 rectangle, so nothing is cropped or distorted on print. What gives is
the property that was wrongly treated as fixed: it is no longer "one page wide."

**Consequence to expect:** a typical engraved part is roughly A4-proportioned at 0.707. At
equal height a Letter sheet is 0.773, so **the Right Page will be slightly wider than one
score page.** That asymmetry is correct. Do not "fix" it.

Rejected: letterboxing a paper-shaped sheet inside the score's height (breaks the flush
band); cropping on print (silently loses markings); scaling on print (distorts
handwriting).

**D2 — Print rules. DECIDED, and they are simpler than a palette swap.**

1. **The paper is never printed.** No background fill is ever laid down. The page ground is
   transparent and the physical sheet's own white is the paper. This works on cream stock,
   on any weight, and it means Stage mode's `#0B0B0D` can never reach a printer, because no
   ground colour ever does.
2. **On a colour printer, the only chromatic ink is the musician's own markings, in the
   colour she authored them in.** Graphite prints graphite. The correction pen prints
   `#C0392B`. The highlighter prints amber, translucent over the engraving.
3. **Therefore: store the authored colour, remap it for display when Stage is on, and print
   the stored colour, never the remap.** This is the rule that makes eng review Issue 9
   coherent. Without it, a bowing written backstage in Stage mode prints chalk-white onto
   white paper and disappears.
4. **The printable-area guide and the sheet's paper-size label never print.** They are
   chrome, not marking, and they follow rule 1.

**D3 — The bottom headroom. DECIDED: non-writable, and it announces itself once.**

Eng review Issue 8 already made it non-writable. The design question left over was
discoverability: it is `alpha = 0` until the paper moves, so a musician who does not think
to scroll cannot reach and mark the lowest system of her part, and will conclude Virtu
cannot mark there. **It reveals itself on first scroll**, then returns to invisible.

It must also stop reading as paper. `ReadingSurfaceView.swift:386-389` currently gives it
the same `#FFFDF8` plate as a page, with a comment arguing that blank paper "reads as
somewhere to write, which is the entire reason it is there." Under the corrected model that
is exactly backwards. It takes the recessed desk field (`Tokens.Paper.wash`, `#E9E5DC`),
no border, no paper texture.

**D4 — The printable-area guide. DECIDED: always visible, low contrast.**

A note written past the printer's non-printable margin is silently clipped, and she only
finds out on paper at a rehearsal. The guide is therefore **permanently drawn** at low but
real contrast, inset from the sheet edge by the printer's non-printable margin
(~6mm / 0.25in, not the ~7% the mockup drew).

Rejected: pencil-proximity reveal. It is the more elegant pattern and it matches the tool
rail's proximity dim, but **there is no pencil hover on macOS**, and macOS is the second
platform. An always-visible guide degrades correctly to every platform on the roadmap.
